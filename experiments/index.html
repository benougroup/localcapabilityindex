SEO/AEO Experiment: Testing Page Characteristics Against Search Visibility

This hub describes a controlled research experiment testing how page characteristics affect search engine discovery, indexing, ranking, and AI retrieval in a low-competition market.

Publication date: 10 September 2026

Observation window: 28 days from publication

Current status: In progress (D0 begins at deployment confirmation)

Purpose

The experiment investigates whether conventional SEO visibility is a prerequisite for AI-enabled search (AEO) visibility, and which page attributes most influence outcomes. We are testing three factors independently:

  • HTML Richness (H): Semantic markup (article tags, breadcrumbs, structured data) vs. basic valid HTML
  • Contextual Depth (C): 350-500 words vs. 900-1300 words with detailed information
  • Internal Links (L): Minimal internal linking vs. multiple discovery pathways

Each factor is tested at low and high levels, creating 8 combinations per topic (2×2×2 design).

The experiment covers two service-discovery topic families in Falkland Islands:

Chinese Tui Na Massage

Research Transparency

This content is part of a controlled experiment. All 16 experimental pages are research pages with synthetic scenarios and research tokens. They are not listings for real providers.

Each page includes:

  • Research token for retrieval testing (e.g., LCI-EXP-T-000)
  • Disclosure of experiment status and factor assignment
  • Publication and review dates
  • Link to this methodology page
  • Safety notices about health information

Observation Schedule

Observations occur at predefined checkpoints:

  • D0 (Publication day): Accessibility and sitemap presence confirmed
  • D1, D3, D7, D14, D28: Search engine crawl, index, and ranking checks
  • Post-index: AI retrieval and citation trials (only for indexed pages)

Results are recorded in an append-only observation log and reported separately from the experimental pages.
